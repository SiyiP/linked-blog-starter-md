
**2 Immunizations that Can be given at Birth Hep B vaccine + RSV Immunization** 
HBV = against hepB
- transmitted sexually, parenterally, and vertically for perinatal transmission
- given at birth due to safety net of protection especially in situations of false negative mother results or if results are misinterpreted, transcripted or reported inaccurately, or simply unavaliable. 
- Chronic HepB infection is the worry; altho  <5% of immunocompetent adults get chronic hepB, **90% of infected infants have their infection become chronic, carrying substantial lifetime risk of cirrhosis, HCC, and premature liver related death.** 

Hep B vaccine - 3 dose series, for children of Hb negative mothers **ideally given @ birth within 24 hrs, 1-2 months, and 6-18 months** 
- AAP notes that when the dose is missed, the vaccine should still be given ASAP rather than omitted. Dose be4 discharge = higher likelihood of completing the full series and remaining up to date on other vaccines 
	- on the occasion the parent is HBsAg-poz or status unknown, both HepB vax and HBIG within 12 hrs of birth is effective
	- <mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">vaccine itself being 75-95% effective in preventing perinatal transmission when given within 24 hrs.</mark> 
	- and <mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">seroconversion rates of 96% = birth dose hepB vax and HBIG within 12 hr of birth </mark>

- in 2025 Dec, Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices **no longer recommends universal birth dose vaccination for infants born to HBsAg- moms,** but shared decision making abt if and when to vax.  
- note that AAP continues to rec birth dose for all infants in spite of ACIP change, so 2 bodies currently differ on whether a routine birth dose's required. 
	- **pediatric cases of acute hep B fell by 99% from 1990 to 2019 after CDC recommended a universal birth dose.**

RSV immunization
- infants born in the first RSV szn and high risk kids thru their 2nd season = single dose of nirsevimab aka beyfortus is recommended for prevention bc it's a frequent cause of lower resp tract dz like bronchiolitis, pneumonia in young kids and older adults 
	- Very common: 2009-2019 according to a nationwide inpatient data sample, RSV was leading cause of hospitalization for patients in the first yr of life
- another way of immunization is through passive immunity, actual RSV vax given 32-36 wks of gestation for passive immunity of infant. typically more than 14 days out from delivery would ensure immunity. 

Nirsevimab = direct monoclonal antibodies
- long half life and potent neutralization. protection for at least 5 months, so only 1 dose is needed.

barriers to vax - 
- "physical" = social factors, meaning barriers to transportation, time of primary care office/outreach, resources, convenience. 
- psychological = related to parental feelings around the vaccine/hesitancy in general 
	- relates to conflicting info - overflow of information in this world ; dis and misinformation from governing bodies and other figures in decision-making process 
	- personal/narrative experiences - anecdotal things can fuel ppl's understanding of risk when looking into vaccinations, etc. 
	- reasoning around risk - low perceived risk of disease esp for those who we have well controlled due to vaccines vs. discomfort and worry over adverse outcomes from when the child is vaccinated.
