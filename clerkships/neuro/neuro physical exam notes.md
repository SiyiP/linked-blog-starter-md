how to do CN tests
1. olfaction - detect and id an aroma in each nostril. ask the patient to block one nostril with a finger, close the eyes sniff ; ask to id an odor. 
2. optic nerve = 
	1. snellen chart one eye at a time = **visual acuity**
	2. color vision color blindness = ask pt to identify a number or shape in the **ishihara plates,** which contain dots of diff color and size 
	3. visual field = assess each eye by using finger, checking quadrants? 
	4. papilla a- fundoscope - using ophthalmoscope - check optic disk and retina 
3. pupillary light reflex, ocolomotor nerve. prompt consensual response. 
	1. eye movement - look back and forth to eval saccades (ability to rapidly fixate eyes from one obj to another). smooth pursuit - slower tracking moements . moving target of trees in car. nystagmus = vergence - align fovea of each eye with targets at different distances, like disconjugate movements. vestibuloocular - stabilize eyes to external world. ask patient to follow finger
	2. paresis = absence of movement in one or both eyes 
	3. alterations in smooth pursuit like saccades 
	4. nystagmus = invol, repetitive movement of one or both eyes 
		1. peripheral nystagmus - jerk nystagmus, **peripheral vestibular system. physiological or path.**
		2. central = jerk or pendular nystagmus = central system. physiological or pathological. rarely with vertigo, can't be suppressed with visual fixation.
	5. direction = vertical - upbeat or downbeat in vestibular nystagmus, horizontal, etc. monocular or binocular? 
	6. physician will movement finger to the patient to test visual accomodation to see constriction of pupil. 
	7. ptosis = check levator palpebrae superioris muscle = patient to open and close their eyes 
	8. oculomotor = innervate **superior rectus, medial rectus, inferior rectus, inf oblique as well as levator palpebrae superioris and ciliary/pupillary sphincter muscle**
4. trochlear nerve = superior oblique muslce - longest course and only one to exit from posterior brainstem portion 
5. trigeminal nerve = facial sensation - light touch 3x places of facial area ask if same on either side. 
	1. also test muscles of mastication - masseters for asymmetry , mandiular branch of trigeminal 
6. abducens - lateral rectus muscle 
7. facial nerve = motor function test with forehead wrinkling, closing eyes tight, nose wrinkling, inflaiton of cheecks, smiling. **sometimes sense of taste too.** 
8. vestibulocochlear nerve
	1. hearing = test with weber and rinne, and doing 
	2. weber = in the middle of forehead. lateralization = asymmetric hearing loss. sound lateralizes to **affected ear in patients with conductive hearing loss, and healthy ear in patients with sensorineural hearing loss**
	3. rinne = will hear under conductive hearing normal ,bc air conduction is greater than bone conduction. but also normal in sensorineural hearing loss , in which bone and air conduction are equally diminished. otherwise, negative test in conductive hearing loss. 
	4. vestibuloocular reflex = head impulse test? actvation of vestibular system, abnormal if they can't maintain central visual fixation (corrective saccade occurs to see target again)
		1. if it's a normal reflex = we see head rotation to right, smooth eye movement to left. 
		2. if it's not normal, we see saccade corrective to the left / right 
	5. sense of balance = check the romberg test, heel to toe walking gait, 
		1. unterberger test? = 
		2. timed up and go test 
		3. tinetti-test = to see in older adults, assess ability to sit, stand upright and then turn 360 degrees. 
9.  glossopharyngeal nerve, palatal movement, involved with 10 as well. ask to open and see soft palate and uvula , pressing down on tongue with stick. 
	1. taste specifically with 9, should taste **bitter substances.**
10. recurrent laryngeal nerve is for vocalization = nerve intact = no hoarseness or bovine cough 
11. traqezius muscle and SCM muscle motor function. test traps with shoulder elevation, sternocleidomastoid with head rotation against resistance 
12. hypoglossal nerve = tongue movements. press tongue against inner cheek with us pushing from outside to test strength. symmetrical and nondeviating tongue when the patient sticks it out too.
